# Copy guardrails — language this skill never writes

**Reviewed by legal: not yet — guidance, not legal advice.** Until legal signs off, this file is a writing standard, not a legal requirement. Any surface that touches auto-renewal, cancellation, price claims or time-limited offers goes to legal before shipping.

Run every option — including non-recommended ones — against this table before delivering `02-copy.md`. An option that breaches a row is rewritten, not flagged.

**Don't duplicate:** jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction cancel-flow law lives in [cancellation.md → Legal context](../../../playbooks/cancellation.md); promotion price and deadline law in [promotions.md → Legal context](../../../playbooks/promotions.md); the Amazon and Adobe cases in [cases.md#ftc-cancellation-enforcement](../../../playbooks/cases.md#ftc-cancellation-enforcement). This file covers *the words*.

## Who these rules protect

| Driver | Meaning | Primary basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Consumer law** | Binding where the buyer is an individual acting outside their business | EU UCPD "consumer" = natural person outside trade or profession ([Dir. 2005/29/EC Art. 2(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02005L0029-20220528)) [Verified]; UK DMCC Act s.225(3) ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/part/4/chapter/1)) [Verified]; California ARL "consumer" = personal, family or household purposes ([BPC §17601](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=17601)) [Verified] |
| **Unclear for B2B** | US federal law — FTC Act §5 and ROSCA ([15 U.S.C. §8403](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/8403)) [Verified] speak of "consumers"; whether small-business buyers are covered was not confirmed here. EU DSA Art. 25 protects all recipients, business users included, but binds only "online platforms" — likely not monday's own SaaS interface (inference; legal to confirm) [Verified — primary text on EUR-Lex, read 2026-09-27] | Don't assume B2B is safe |
| **Trust (monday policy)** | Applies to every buyer, B2B included, because monday's own design system forbids manipulating or guilting users ([Vibe values](https://github.com/mondaycom/vibe/blob/master/packages/docs/src/pages/foundations/ux-writing-handbook/values/valuesUXWriting.tsx)) [Verified], and because monday sells to individuals too | Every row below |

monday sells to both individuals and teams, and one surface usually serves both. **Write every line to the consumer standard.**

## Guardrails

Taxonomy sources: [FTC staff report *Bringing Dark Patterns to Light*, Sept 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/bringing-dark-patterns-light) (Appendix A, pp.21–26) [Verified]; [Mathur et al. 2019, *Dark Patterns at Scale*, doi:10.1145/3359183](https://doi.org/10.1145/3359183) — 1,818 dark-pattern instances on 1,254 of ~11K shopping sites, 15 types in 7 categories [Verified]; [CMA *Online Choice Architecture*, CMA155, Apr 2022](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-choice-architecture-how-digital-design-can-harm-competition-and-consumers) [Verified]; [deceptive.design types](https://www.deceptive.design/types) (Brignull) [Verified]. Staff reports, discussion papers and taxonomies are guidance, not law; the statutes and the Amazon order are the binding texts (the order binds only Amazon, but shows what the FTC accepts).

| # | Never write | Pattern name | Why (source, tag) | Driver | Surfaces | Write instead (slot pattern) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | A decline that shames the user: "No thanks, I don't want to save money", "No, I prefer doing it manually", "I don't care about my team's productivity" | Confirmshaming | FTC 2022 names it, with a near-identical "save money" example (p.25) [Verified]; Mathur et al. counted 169 instances [Verified]; the Amazon order bars a decline reading "no thanks, I don't want free shipping" (§V(A)) [Verified — binds Amazon]; Vibe value "keep it stress-free" [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 | `No thanks` · `Not now` · `Stay on {current_plan}` · `Continue to cancel` |
| G2 | "Offer ends soon", "Last chance", "Today only" without a real end; any countdown that resets | Fake urgency / baseless countdown | FTC 2022 p.22 [Verified]; UK DMCC Act Sch. 20 para 7 — falsely claiming limited-time availability ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20)) [Verified]; EU UCPD Annex I pt 7 [Verified]; Mathur et al.: 157 of 393 timers deceptive [Verified]. Details: promotions.md | Consumer law + Trust | 3, 7, 1 | `Offer ends {date} at {time} {tz}` — only with a real end date in the spec |
| G3 | "Only {N} spots left", "Selling fast", "{N} teams upgraded in the last hour" — unless measured, current and true | Fake scarcity / false activity | FTC 2022 pp.21–22 [Verified]; DMCC Sch. 20 para 20 (materially inaccurate market or availability info) [Verified]; CMA155 ¶3.18 treats fake scarcity and popularity claims as particularly harmful [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 3 | Real, sourced numbers with a date: `{N} teams on {plan} use {feature}` (from data, stated in the spec) |
| G4 | A headline price that excludes mandatory costs: per-seat price without the seat minimum, "from ${X}" when required add-ons apply, fees first shown at checkout | Drip pricing / hidden costs | FTC 2022 p.9: mandatory fees belong in the upfront price [Verified]; DMCC Act s.230(2)(b), (4): total price is material info and includes fees the consumer will necessarily incur ([s.230](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/section/230)) [Verified]; UCPD Art. 7(4)(c) [Verified]; CMA155 marks drip pricing usually harmful [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 4, 5 | `${bundle_total}/mo for {N} seats` · `${price}/seat/mo · {min_seats}-seat minimum · ${total}/mo` |
| G5 | A per-period price that hides the commitment — "${X}/mo" for a plan billed annually up front, with no total | Price comparison prevention | FTC 2022 p.22 lists per-payment prices shown without the number of payments or total [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 3, 4 | `${per_month}/seat/mo, billed annually (${annual_total})` |
| G6 | "Free" when anything must be paid — a "free" trial that converts to paid without saying so, "free credits" that require a paid plan | Misleading "free" | UCPD Annex I pt 20 [Verified]; DMCC Sch. 20 para 23 [Verified]; FTC 2022 pp.10–11 on trials that roll into charges [Verified]; CA ARL: trial offers must state the post-trial price clearly and conspicuously (§17602(a)(1)) [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 7, 3, 5 | `Free for {trial_length} days — no card needed` (only as true per monday-context) · `{N} credits included with {plan}` |
| G7 | An auto-renewing plan's CTA or sign-up screen that doesn't say it renews, for how much and how to stop — or pushes those terms into a T&C link | Hidden subscription / forced continuity | ROSCA §8403: clear disclosure of material terms before billing info, express informed consent, simple way to stop [Verified]; CA ARL: renewal terms clear and conspicuous, in visual proximity to the consent request (§17602(a)(1), (4)) [Verified]; Amazon order §V(D)–(E): say "renews" and show price on every sign-up page [Verified — binds Amazon]; FTC 2022 p.14: accepting general terms isn't consent to a charge [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 3, 4, 7 | Beside the CTA: `{plan} renews every {period} at ${price} until you cancel. Cancel anytime in {location}.` |
| G8 | A button that does more than its label — "Continue", "Next", "Try it" or "Get started" that charges, enrolls or upgrades | Disguised action / unauthorized charge | FTC 2022 p.24: a "Next" that processes a purchase [Verified]; Amazon order §V(B): the CTA must name what's joined [Verified — binds Amazon] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 4, 5, 7 | `Pay ${total} and upgrade` · `Start {plan} — ${price}/{period}` (copy-craft C3) |
| G9 | Double negatives or labels whose outcome is ambiguous — "Don't cancel my subscription?", a "No, cancel" button that exits the flow and *keeps* the plan | Trick wording | FTC 2022 p.25 [Verified]; deceptive.design trick wording [Verified]; EU Commission UCPD guidance (2021) names double negatives [Reported — primary §4.2.7 not loaded] | Consumer law + Trust | 6, 4, 2 | Labels map one-to-one to outcomes: `Cancel subscription` / `Keep subscription` |
| G10 | The way out worded as body text or a faint afterthought — "or continue with fewer features", "maybe later" — while the stay option is a button | False hierarchy / visual interference | FTC 2022 pp.23–24 [Verified]; DSA Art. 25(3)(a) prominence [Verified]; CA ARL §17602(e)(2): a save offer only with a "click to cancel" option shown at the same time [Verified] — rules in cancellation.md | Consumer law + Trust | 6, 2, 4 | The decline is a named button: `Continue to cancel` · `Stay on {current_plan}` |
| G11 | Copy that stretches cancellation: more than one "Are you sure?", a chain of offers, "Contact us to cancel" when signup was self-serve | Obstruction / hard to cancel | ROSCA §8403(3) [Verified]; FTC 2022 pp.14–15: cancel at least as easy as sign-up, same medium [Verified]; FTC v. Amazon ("Iliad") — see cases.md; DSA Art. 25(3)(c) [Verified]; Amazon order §IV [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 6 | `Cancel {plan}` reachable in the account; one offer screen with `Continue to cancel` beside it (cancellation.md flow) |
| G12 | A recurring prompt whose only decline is "Not now" / "Maybe later" | Nagging | FTC 2022 p.24 gives "Yes / Not now" instead of "Yes / No" as nagging [Verified]; DSA Art. 25(3)(b) repeated requests [Verified] | Trust (+ consumer) | 2, 4, 5, 7 | `Not now` on first show; once the prompt reaches its compact or re-show threshold (owned by paywalls.md *Timing and frequency*), add `Don't show again` |
| G13 | Pre-checked paid add-ons or auto top-up switched on by default, or a promotion that silently changes a billing setting | Sneak-into-basket / preselection | FTC 2022 pp.22–23, 25 [Verified]; CMA155 lists defaults in its taxonomy [Verified]; promotions.md: a promotion must never change a billing setting as a side effect | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 3, 5 | Unchecked, priced and explicit: `Turn on auto top-up: {N} credits (${price}) when balance reaches {threshold}` |
| G14 | An inflated or invented "was" price, or a "sale" that is the normal price | False discount / misleading reference price | FTC 2022 p.22 [Verified]; DMCC s.226 misleading actions (see promotions.md) [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 3, 1, 6 | `~~${list}~~ ${offer}` where `{list}` is the genuine selling price (copy-craft N6) |
| G15 | Testimonials, ratings or counts you can't source — "Loved by teams everywhere", invented quotes, "Most popular" without data | Fake social proof | FTC 2022 p.21 [Verified]; UCPD Annex I pts 23b–23c [Verified]; DMCC Sch. 20 para 13 [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 3 | Attributed and real: `"{quote}" — {name}, {role} at {company}`; company-level counts only from monday-context |
| G16 | Consequences that are exaggerated or untrue — "You'll lose everything", "Your data will be deleted" when it won't, security scares to sell | Misleading action / scare | UCPD Art. 6: presentation that deceives even if factually accurate [Verified]; DMCC Sch. 20 para 14 (materially inaccurate claims about security risks) [Verified]; FTC 2022 p.6: companies are judged on the net impression [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 6, 7, 4, 5 | Exact consequence, date and what's kept: `On {date}, {N} automations stop. Your boards and data stay.` |
| G17 | Guilt, pity or the company's livelihood — "Don't leave your team behind", "We're a small team and we need you" | Emotional pressure | DMCC Sch. 20 para 32 (telling the consumer the trader's job or livelihood is at risk) [Verified]; Vibe values — no guilt, no passive-aggressive language [Verified]; cancellation.md anti-pattern | Trust (+ consumer) | 6, 4, 7 | Acknowledge and inform: `Your plan stays active until {date}. You won't be charged again.` |
| G18 | "Unlimited AI" (or unlimited anything) when fair-use caps, credit limits or throttling apply | Hidden information | FTC 2022 pp.6–8: net impression; material terms not hidden [Verified]; UCPD Art. 7 misleading omission [Verified] | Consumer law + Trust | 1, 2, 5 | `Includes {N} AI credits/mo · then {overage_rule}` (terms from monday-context) |
| G19 | Unsubstantiated superlatives — "the #1 work platform", "the most awarded" | Hype claim | monday brand voice: no overpromising, no unsubstantiated superlatives ([brand-monday.com](https://www.brand-monday.com/tone-of-voice)) [Verified]; comparative-claim rules vary by market — flag for legal | Trust | 1, 3 | Specific proof with a source: `{N} customers` (monday-context) · a named badge with date |

## Pre-delivery check (every option, not just ★)

1. Does any decline label judge the user? (G1)
2. Is every deadline a real date in the spec? Every scarcity or popularity number sourced? (G2, G3, G15)
3. Does the number the eye lands on equal what the user will pay, with the commitment on the same line? (G4, G5)
4. Does every paid CTA say what it charges and that it renews? (G7, G8)
5. Does each label mean exactly one outcome, and is the way out a named button? (G9, G10)
6. Is every consequence true, specific and dated? (G16)
